0. YOUR NARRATIVE ARC (Start Here)

This is your story. It's honest, strategic, and shows you've thought deeply about this market. Lead with this in the interview.

The Skeptic Who Found Conviction

Part 1: Skepticism

"I was originally skeptical of AI platforms. At GitHub, I saw the unit economics up close — Copilot was losing money on every user because of token costs. The model layer is a race to the bottom with unsustainable economics. I watched a lot of AI companies burning cash on inference with no path to profitability."

Part 2: The Karp Framework Changed My Mind

"But then I started thinking about Karp's framework — models, data, and infrastructure are all commoditizing. The value is migrating to the orchestration layer. That's where Dify sits. And unlike the model layer, the orchestration layer can have sustainable unit economics because you're not paying for inference — you're providing the workflow infrastructure that connects commoditized models to real business workflows."

Part 3: Why I Reached Out

"That's why I reached out to Dify. I see three opportunities in this layer:

1. New narrative: AI enables businesses, doesn't replace them. Dify's Human Input Node is exactly this — humans and agents working together, not agents replacing humans. That's the narrative enterprises need to hear.

2. Model-agnostic = cost-optimized: By being agnostic about models, Dify lets enterprises pick the cheapest model for each task. As token costs become a real concern for enterprises, this is a massive advantage over locked-in platforms like Microsoft Copilot Studio or Google Vertex AI.

3. Open-source innovation: The Creator Center and Template Marketplace mean the community builds the ecosystem. Dify doesn't need to build everything — it needs to be the platform others build on. That's the GitHub playbook."

Part 4: The Global Opportunity

"And there's global demand for this. Every enterprise in every market is trying to figure out how to operationalize AI. The ones that succeed won't be the ones with the best models — they'll be the ones with the best orchestration. Dify can be that platform globally."

6. Interview Answer Frameworks

"Why Dify?"

"I was skeptical of AI platforms. At GitHub, I saw unsustainable unit economics — Copilot losing money on every user. But Karp's framework changed my mind: models and infrastructure are commoditizing, and value is moving to the orchestration layer. That's Dify. And unlike the model layer, the orchestration layer can have sustainable economics. That's why I reached out."

"What's Dify's product vision?"

"The operating system for AI-native organizations. Today it's a workflow builder. Tomorrow it's the platform every team uses to build and manage AI agents. The Creator Center and Marketplace are the first steps — turning Dify from a tool into an ecosystem."

"How would you expand Dify's market?"

"Four vectors: geographic (Western enterprise — SOC 2, GDPR already done), horizontal (developers → IT leaders → business users via Human Input Node), vertical (double down on logistics, pharma, professional services), and partner (system integrators in APAC, developer advocacy in the West)."

"How do you think about competition?"

"The landscape is consolidating — Workday acquired Flowise and Pipedream, Asana acquired Stack AI. Dify is one of the last independent open-source AI platforms with enterprise traction. Our moat: open-source trust, model-agnostic, production-grade. And the model-agnostic piece is key — as token costs become a concern, enterprises will want to pick the cheapest model for each task, not be locked into Microsoft or Google."

"How would you execute GTM?"

"GitHub's playbook: generous free tier drives adoption, developers bring it to work, compliance pulls companies to enterprise, marketplace creates lock-in. Dify's sandbox (200 messages/mo) might be too restrictive — GitHub's generous free tier drove 180M users. I'd test a more generous free tier and measure the conversion impact."

"Why should we hire you?"

"Three reasons: (1) I've watched this exact playbook work at GitHub — open-source to enterprise to acquisition. (2) I bridge China R&D and Western enterprise — that's my entire career. (3) I have a genuine point of view on Dify's strategy — the orchestration layer thesis, the model-agnostic cost advantage, the GitHub parallel. I'm not just looking for a job — I have conviction about this specific opportunity."
