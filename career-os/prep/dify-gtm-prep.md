## What Dify Actually Does

**In plain English:** Dify lets companies build AI-powered workflows without writing code from scratch. It sits between AI models (GPT, Claude, Llama, etc.) and business systems (CRM, databases, support tools).

**Example use cases:**
- Maersk: Document review pipelines
- ETS: NLP pipelines for assessment products
- Volvo: Rapid AI validation for automotive
- RICOH: Citizen development (non-technical employees building AI tools)
- Novartis, Deloitte, McDonald's, PayPal, Adobe, Google: Various enterprise workflows

**Core product:**
- **Workflow Studio** — Visual drag-and-drop builder for AI agent workflows
- **Agent** — Build AI agents with tool-calling, reasoning, memory
- **Knowledge Pipeline** — RAG (connect AI to company's own data)
- **Plugins** — Extensible marketplace for tools and integrations
- **Publish & Monitor** — Deploy as web apps, APIs, or chat widgets

## Interview Prep: "Product Vision & Market Expansion Strategy"

The CFO (who is a "seasoned investor") wants to hear your thinking on:

### Topic 1: Product Vision

**What they want to know:** Do you understand what Dify is building and where it's going?

**Your answer framework:**

> "Dify is building the operating system for AI-native organizations. Today it's a workflow builder. Tomorrow it's the platform every team uses to build, deploy, and manage their AI agents — like how every team uses Slack for communication and Notion for documentation.
>
> The key shift is from 'developers building AI apps' to 'every team building AI workflows.' The Creator Center and Template Marketplace (launched May 2026) are the first steps — turning Dify from a tool into an ecosystem.
>
> The vision is: Many People. Many Agents. One Simple Way to Work."

**Key talking points:**
- The shift from chatbot → agent → agentic workflow is the biggest paradigm change in AI
- Dify's positioning between models and business systems is the right layer to own
- The template marketplace creates network effects (more templates → more users → more creators)
- Enterprise adoption is the revenue engine; open-source is the distribution engine

### Topic 2: Market Expansion Strategy

**What they want to know:** How would you expand Dify's market?

**Your answer framework:**

**A. Geographic expansion:**
> "Dify is strong in China and APAC. The next frontier is Western enterprise — specifically the US and Europe. The challenge is trust: Western enterprises need to see local reference customers, local support, and compliance certifications. Dify already has SOC 2, ISO 27001, and GDPR — that's the foundation. Now it needs Western enterprise case studies and a local GTM team."

**B. Horizontal expansion:**
> "Dify started with developers. The blog content now targets IT leaders and marketers. That's the right expansion — from technical builder to business user. The Human Input Node (v1.13.0) is key: it lets non-technical users participate in AI workflows. That's how you go from 'developer tool' to 'enterprise platform.'"

**C. Vertical expansion:**
> "Dify has logos in logistics (Maersk), pharma (Novartis), automotive (Volvo), education (ETS), and professional services (Deloitte). The next move is to double down on 2-3 verticals with dedicated solutions. Healthcare, financial services, and legal are high-value verticals where AI workflows have massive demand but strict compliance requirements."

**D. Partner ecosystem:**
> "The Reseller, Service Partner, and Marketplace Partner programs are the right channel strategy. In APAC, system integrators and consulting firms are the fastest path to enterprise adoption. In the West, it's developer advocacy and PLG. Different motions for different markets."

### Topic 3: Competitive Strategy

**What they want to know:** How do you think about competition?

**Your answer:**

> "Dify's moat is three things:
> 1. **Open-source trust** — Enterprises can self-host, audit the code, avoid vendor lock-in. Microsoft and Google can't offer that.
> 2. **Model-agnostic** — Works with any LLM. As the model landscape fragments (OpenAI, Anthropic, open-source), being model-agnostic becomes more valuable, not less.
> 3. **Production-grade** — SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, VPC deployment. Dify is the only open-source AI platform that's enterprise-ready out of the box.
>
> The risk is commoditization. If the workflow builder becomes a commodity, Dify needs to own the ecosystem — templates, plugins, integrations. That's where the Creator Center and Marketplace come in. The platform that attracts the most creators wins."

### Topic 4: GTM Execution

**What they want to know:** How would you actually execute GTM?

**Your answer:**

> "Three motions:
>
> **1. PLG (Product-Led Growth):** Optimize the free → paid conversion. The sandbox tier has 200 messages/month — that's enough to build a POC but not enough for production. Push users to Professional ($590/yr) with in-app prompts, usage dashboards, and success stories.
>
> **2. Enterprise sales:** Build a small enterprise sales team (starting in APAC, then US). Target IT leaders, not developers. The blog content ('From AI Experiment to Enterprise Platform') is already doing the thought leadership. Now convert that into pipeline.
>
> **3. Partner channel:** Activate the Reseller and Service Partner programs. In APAC, partner with system integrators (Accenture, Deloitte, PwC — all already using Dify). In the US, partner with AI consultancies.
>
> Key metric: open-source → cloud conversion rate. Industry average is 2-5%. If Dify can get to 3%, that's 42,000 paying cloud customers from 1.4M installations."
